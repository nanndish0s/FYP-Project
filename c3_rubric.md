# C3 Skill Rubric (Curiosity, Critical Thinking, Creativity)

**Context**: AI Recruitment for Software Engineering Candidates  
**Application**: Evaluating soft skills during technical interviews

**Academic Foundation**: This rubric is grounded in established psychometric instruments, competency frameworks for technical hiring, and creativity assessment methodologies, aligning with industry best practices for behavioral assessment in software engineering recruitment.

---

## 1. Curiosity
*Definition*: The desire to learn, understand, and explore new information or possibilities.

**Academic Basis**: Based on Litman & Spielberger's Epistemic Curiosity Scale (ECS), differentiating between diverse curiosity (broad exploration) and specific curiosity (knowledge gap closure) [1][2]. Validated for software engineering recruitment contexts [3][4].

| Score | Level | Description | Indicators | Research Support |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Passive / Indifferent** | Shows no interest in the topic; gives minimal, closed responses. | "I don't know.", "It is what it is." | Low epistemic curiosity [1] |
| **2** | **Low Interest** | Answers the question but seeks no further depth. | Standard answers, no follow-up questions. | Lack of proactive learning [3] |
| **3** | **Moderate Curiosity** | Shows engagement; asks basic clarifying questions. | "How does that work?", "Can you explain more?" | Diverse curiosity - broad exploration [1][2] |
| **4** | **High Curiosity** | Actively seeks to understand the "why" and "how"; explores implications. | "Why did they choose that approach?", "What happens if we change X?" | High diverse + specific curiosity [1][3] |
| **5** | **Deep / Epistemic Curiosity** | Probes underlying mechanisms; challenges assumptions; seeks to bridge gaps in knowledge. | "This connects to [Theory X]... I wonder if [Hypothesis Y] would solve the limitation?" | Epistemic curiosity - knowledge gap closure [1][2][4] |

**Assessment Methods**: Behavioral interviews, situational judgment tests, project review [3][4][5]

---

## 2. Critical Thinking
*Definition*: The objective analysis and evaluation of an issue in order to form a judgment.

**Academic Basis**: Aligned with critical thinking competency frameworks for software engineers [6][7], incorporating problem decomposition, logical reasoning, evaluative judgment, and synthesis capabilities validated in technical hiring contexts [8][9].

| Score | Level | Description | Indicators | Research Support |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Uncritical** | Accepts information blindly; relies on gut feeling or clichés without evidence. | "It just feels right.", "Everyone does it." | Absence of analytical thinking [6][7] |
| **2** | **Basic Understanding** | Can describe the issue but lacks analysis; misses obvious flaws. | Restates the problem without dissecting it. | Descriptive vs. analytical [6][8] |
| **3** | **Competent Analysis** | Identifies main pros/cons; follows a logical structure. | "On one hand X, on the other hand Y." | Problem decomposition [6][7][9] |
| **4** | **Strong Evaluation** | Identifies biases, hidden assumptions, and edge cases; evaluates evidence quality. | "The argument assumes X, which might not be true in case Y." | Evaluative judgment [6][7][8] |
| **5** | **Advanced Synthesis** | Deconstructs complex systems; synthesizes multiple viewpoints; proposes evidence-based solutions. | "The root cause isn't X, it's the interaction between Y and Z. Therefore, we should..." | Systems thinking + synthesis [6][7][9] |

**Assessment Methods**: Cognitive assessments (CCAT), structured technical interviews, scenario-based questions [8][9][10]

---

## 3. Creativity
*Definition*: The use of the imagination or original ideas, especially in the production of an artistic work or solution.

**Academic Basis**: Incorporates dimensions from OECD Creativity Rubric [11], VALUE Rubric for Creative Thinking [12], and Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking (TTCT) [13][14], measuring fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration, and transformative thinking.

| Score | Level | Description | Indicators | Research Support |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Conventional** | Stick strictly to standard rules/procedures; no deviation. | "This is the standard way." | No creative divergence [11][13] |
| **2** | **Incremental** | Minor variations on existing ideas; safe choices. | "We could change the color." | Fluency - basic idea generation [13][14] |
| **3** | **Adaptive** | Adapts existing solutions to new contexts; shows some flexibility. | "Like Uber, but for [X]." | Flexibility - variety of approaches [11][13][14] |
| **4** | **Original** | Proposes novel ideas; connects unrelated concepts (divergent thinking). | "What if we completely removed the interface?" | Originality + elaboration [11][12][13] |
| **5** | **Transformative** | Paradigm-shifting ideas; redefines the problem space; high novelty and utility. | "Instead of solving X, we should eliminate the need for X entirely by doing Y." | Transformative creativity [11][12][15] |

**Assessment Methods**: Innovation-enhanced rubrics, creative coding assessments, consensual assessment technique (CAT) [13][14][15][16]

---

## Soft Skills in Technical Hiring Context

Research demonstrates that soft skills are ranked **as highly as technical skills** by employers in software engineering recruitment [17][18], with long-term job success significantly tied to soft skills mastery [18]. This rubric integrates behavioral competencies validated in technical hiring practices [17][19][20].

**Assessment Best Practices**:
- Behavioral interviews using STAR method [20][21]
- Structured interviews with consistent scoring [19][20]
- Competency-based evaluation frameworks [19][21]
- Observable behavioral indicators [17][18][20]
