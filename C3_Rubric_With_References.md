## 1. CURIOSITY RUBRIC

**Academic Foundation**: Litman & Spielberger Epistemic Curiosity Scale (ECS) [1,2]; Software Engineering Recruitment Assessment [3,4]

| Score | Level | Description | Behavioral Indicators | Research Support |
|:------|:------|:------------|:---------------------|:-----------------|
| **1** | **Passive / Indifferent** | Shows no interest in the topic; gives minimal, closed responses | "I don't know.", "It is what it is." | Low epistemic curiosity; minimal information-seeking behavior (Litman & Spielberger, 2003) [1] |
| **2** | **Low Interest** | Answers the question but seeks no further depth | Standard answers, no follow-up questions | Lack of proactive learning; no self-directed knowledge pursuit (Testlify, 2024) [5] |
| **3** | **Moderate Curiosity** | Shows engagement; asks basic clarifying questions | "How does that work?", "Can you explain more?" | **Diverse curiosity** - broad information exploration (Litman & Spielberger, 2003) [1]; moderate learning agility (Practice Aptitude Tests, 2024) [4] |
| **4** | **High Curiosity** | Actively seeks to understand the "why" and "how"; explores implications | "Why did they choose that approach?", "What happens if we change X?" | High diverse + **specific curiosity** - resolving knowledge gaps (Litman & Spielberger, 2003) [1]; proactive learning in tech contexts (Yardstick, 2024) [3] |
| **5** | **Deep / Epistemic Curiosity** | Probes underlying mechanisms; challenges assumptions; seeks to bridge gaps in knowledge | "This connects to [Theory X]... I wonder if [Hypothesis Y] would solve the limitation?" | **Epistemic curiosity** - knowledge gap closure and integration (Litman & Spielberger, 2003; JST, 2024) [1,2]; intellectual drive for continuous learning (Alooba, 2024) [6] |

**Psychometric Validation**: Cronbach's α ≈ 0.81 for ECS dimensions [2]

## 2. CRITICAL THINKING RUBRIC

**Academic Foundation**: Critical Thinking Competency Frameworks for Software Engineers [7,8]; Cognitive Assessment Standards [9,10]

| Score | Level | Description | Behavioral Indicators | Research Support |
|:------|:------|:------------|:---------------------|:-----------------|
| **1** | **Uncritical** | Accepts information blindly; relies on gut feeling or clichés without evidence | "It just feels right.", "Everyone does it." | Absence of analytical thinking; no evidence evaluation (Testlify, 2024) [7] |
| **2** | **Basic Understanding** | Can describe the issue but lacks analysis; misses obvious flaws | Restates the problem without dissecting it | **Descriptive vs. analytical** thinking; surface-level comprehension (BytePerceptions, 2024) [8] |
| **3** | **Competent Analysis** | Identifies main pros/cons; follows a logical structure | "On one hand X, on the other hand Y." | **Problem decomposition**; structured reasoning (Testlify, 2024; EzyHire, 2024) [7,9] |
| **4** | **Strong Evaluation** | Identifies biases, hidden assumptions, and edge cases; evaluates evidence quality | "The argument assumes X, which might not be true in case Y." | **Evaluative judgment**; recognizes cognitive biases and logical fallacies (Testlify, 2024; BytePerceptions, 2024) [7,8] |
| **5** | **Advanced Synthesis** | Deconstructs complex systems; synthesizes multiple viewpoints; proposes evidence-based solutions | "The root cause isn't X, it's the interaction between Y and Z. Therefore, we should..." | **Systems thinking + synthesis**; integrates diverse perspectives into coherent solutions (Testlify, 2024; Sprad.io, 2024) [7,11] |

**Industry Validation**: Used in Criteria Cognitive Aptitude Test (CCAT) for software engineers [10]

## 3. CREATIVITY RUBRIC

**Academic Foundation**: OECD Creativity Rubric [12]; VALUE Rubric for Creative Thinking [13]; Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking (TTCT) [14,15]

| Score | Level | Description | Behavioral Indicators | Research Support |
|:------|:------|:------------|:---------------------|:-----------------|
| **1** | **Conventional** | Stick strictly to standard rules/procedures; no deviation | "This is the standard way." | No creative divergence; adherence to norms (OECD, 2019; TechLifeFuture, 2024) [12,15] |
| **2** | **Incremental** | Minor variations on existing ideas; safe choices | "We could change the color." | **Fluency** - basic idea generation; minimal novelty (Torrance TTCT; Braden Kelley, 2024) [14,15] |
| **3** | **Adaptive** | Adapts existing solutions to new contexts; shows some flexibility | "Like Uber, but for [X]." | **Flexibility** - variety of approaches; adaptive thinking (OECD, 2019; Torrance TTCT) [12,14] |
| **4** | **Original** | Proposes novel ideas; connects unrelated concepts (divergent thinking) | "What if we completely removed the interface?" | **Originality + elaboration**; divergent thinking and idea refinement (OECD, 2019; UC Berkeley VALUE Rubric, 2024) [12,13] |
| **5** | **Transformative** | Paradigm-shifting ideas; redefines the problem space; high novelty and utility | "Instead of solving X, we should eliminate the need for X entirely by doing Y." | **Transformative creativity**; redefines problem domains (OECD, 2019; UC Berkeley VALUE Rubric; ResearchGate SEREBRO) [12,13,16] |

**Validation**: Used by software companies for innovation labs; multi-dimensional assessment (fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration) [14,15]
